# Skills-gap variable audit — S1

This is an inventory and routing audit, not a skills-gap analysis. The extension
defines skills gap as: "Skills gap, as measurable from the LFS: the difference in
education and vocational-training attainment between unemployed and employed youth,
the education mismatch of employed youth relative to the occupations they hold,
and what happens to youth at each education level in the labour market. Education
and training are proxies for skills. Vacancies and employer demand are not measured
in the LFS; employer surveys (NAVTTC/NSIS) are cited as context only."

Sources: PBS [2024–25 questionnaire](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Questionnaire-of-LFS-2024-25-Final.pdf),
[coding scheme](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/CODING-SCHEME-LFS-2024-25.pdf),
[enumerator manual](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/MANUAL-of-LFS2024-25-1.pdf),
[course codes](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/TechnicalVocational-Training-Course-Codes.pdf),
[occupation classification](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/wp-content/uploads/2020/07/Occuptions-Classification-4-digits.pdf),
[PBS LFS methodology and publications](https://www.pbs.gov.pk/labour-force-statistics/).
The raw Stata file and questionnaire were inspected locally; the other official
linked PBS publications were inspected online. Counts are generated by
`python scripts/09_skills_audit.py`. The exhaustive observed-category counts
and their sample sizes and weighted totals are in
[skills_audit_counts.csv](../reports/tables/skills_audit_counts.csv); raw Stata
value labels are in [skills_audit_stata_codebook.csv](../reports/tables/skills_audit_stata_codebook.csv).
Occupation-by-sex-and-area counts are in
[skills_audit_occupation_groups.csv](../reports/tables/skills_audit_occupation_groups.csv).

## Population, missingness and disclosure

All-youth counts include the whole age range, including those outside the labour
force. The employed and unemployed groups use the previously approved label
in `scripts/01_build_dataset.py`, excluding the approved ambiguous absence
records. `Weights` supplies population totals; totals are rounded to whole
persons and n is unweighted. The tables suppress every cell with n < 30 and
mark 30–49 as unreliable. A suppressed cell can include zero. Missing data
are labelled “missing / not asked”: a blank on a routed question is not evidence
of a negative answer. The local coding scheme documents geography and does not
document general item-specific numeric missing codes; do not invent missing
codes. Observed numeric values and Stata labels are listed in the tables; values
outside questionnaire codes require investigation. Detailed occupation and
industry codes are summarised as recorded/missing, not individual sparse codes.
Survey weights are estimates, not model features.

Empirical routing and age checks are saved in
[`skills_audit_routing.csv`](../reports/tables/skills_audit_routing.csv) and
[`skills_audit_education_ages.csv`](../reports/tables/skills_audit_education_ages.csv).
Occupation recorded outside the approved employment group is confined to a
suppressed cell and is discarded for the employed occupation lens. Training
type and provider are routed from the training question. Missing or invalid
enrolment/education responses remain missing rather than being forced into a
category. An age distribution can identify implausible patterns but cannot
prove individual coding.

The approved exclusion applies across all ages: code 14 is excluded, as is a
missing reason for a person marked absent from work. Only a subset occurs among
youth, accounting for the five-record youth-versus-all-ages distinction in the
earlier audit. These are not five additional exclusions. The verified
classification comparison uses the published employed population aged 10+.

The separate industry lens is excluded because the official industry code
document could not be verified. A detailed field-of-study lens is excluded
because education codes only identify a few broad fields. The optional
unemployment-barriers lens is excluded because non-search reasons are not
general reasons for failing to find work. `Fstatus` is unused.

- `S9C10`'s questionnaire question concerns prior paid or income work, but
  the Stata variable label says registered with a private employment agency.
  The actual agency-search field is `S9C210`. The routing rows in the count
  table compare 9.10 codes with whether previous occupation is recorded; this
  supports the questionnaire interpretation, but is not proof of every record.
- `S5C5` absence reason has the previously investigated shifted Stata labels;
  the team approved questionnaire coding and exclusion of ambiguous responses.
  See `docs/label_definition.md` and `docs/DECISIONS.md`; no label rule changes.
- The questionnaire includes a third sex category; the Stata value labels only
  describe male and female. Check observed values in the count table; do not
  recode a third category into either sex.
- The questionnaire names Annex C for course type, while Stata supplies no
  `S4C12` value labels. PBS publishes the linked list separately. Approval is
  still required before any training-type analysis.
- PBS's overview calls the occupation classification PSCO 2015; the actual
  linked four-digit classification document is titled PSCO 2012. Both refer to
  an ISCO-08-aligned structure but this version discrepancy needs PBS/team
  verification. The occupation PDF and data should not be represented as a
  conclusively verified 2015 codebook. The linked industry classification
  document could not be retrieved in this audit; PBS describes PSIC 2010 based
  on ISIC revision 4. Four-digit industry labels remain subject to verification.
- The questionnaire has inconsistent-looking transfer instructions around the
  later previous-job page. Follow the explicit 9.10 skip and compare routing
  empirically; do not assume the page header settles the ambiguity.
- `S4C9` degree subjects are broad categories; different degrees and completion
  levels do not form a fully ordered skills scale. The Stata labels and PBS
  wording should be checked alongside the five variables at Gate S1.

## Proposals for Gate S1 — not approved

- Proposed five-band mapping consistent with the existing test-evaluation
  grouping: 1 = no formal; 2–5 = below matric; 6 = matric; 7 = intermediate;
  8–16 = degree or above. The plan calls 2–5 “primary or middle”, but 2–3 are
  below primary; the more accurate label is “below matric”. Unknown or missing
  stays unknown. Team approval is required.
- Use the first digit of a valid four-digit main-occupation code as the proposed
  ISCO-style major group; do not treat missing as group zero, and exclude armed
  forces after verifying the applicable local classification. Two-digit
  breakdowns are only candidates where disclosure rules permit them.
- For the proposed normative sensitivity, ISCO-08 associates major group 1
  with levels 3–4, group 2 with 4, group 3 with 3, groups 4–8 with 2, and group
  9 with 1; armed forces span levels and should be excluded. The plan proposes
  level 1 up to primary, level 2 middle/matric/intermediate, and levels 3–4
  degree or above. A combined 2–5 band cannot distinguish primary from middle;
  a direct code-level rule or a revised grouping needs explicit team approval.
- Potentially supported: education/training attainment, employed occupation
  distribution, education-to-occupation proxy mismatch, and outcomes using the
  approved norm populations. Previous-job questions are conditional and
  sparse; preferred work and non-search reasons have different denominators.
  NEET needs all youth, employment status plus current schooling and current
  training, with missing values unresolved; neither the labour-force-only
  modelling dataset nor the ever-trained flag alone is sufficient. No vacancies,
  employer demand, directly measured competencies or comprehensive job-finding
  barriers are available from these fields.
